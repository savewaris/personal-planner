# Restructure your planner around Areas, Focus, and approved AI plans

Give each concept exactly one job: Areas and Projects are containers, tags are context, and board columns are state. That separation is what PARA, GTD and Things 3 converge on, and it is the fix for the six confusing group-by modes. An Area is an ongoing responsibility with "no finish line" and a Project is a finite outcome ([Forte Labs PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)). Things 3 lets a to-do live in a project, directly in an area, or in the Inbox, which matches your three task homes exactly ([Things docs](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803573/)). A single Focus board (Today / In Progress / Up Next / Someday / Done) then becomes one status enum on Task, with area and project shown as chips and filters. For capture, Todoist Ramble is the clearest propose-then-approve precedent: tasks appear in a preview and "Nothing is final until you ... click Add tasks" ([Todoist help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/use-ramble-to-add-tasks-by-voice-P1Raq7vVF)). The recommended build is a flat, schema-validated AI proposal stored as a draft, approved item by item, then committed. Expose the same propose/apply pair through a bearer-token MCP route in your Next.js app first, and add OAuth only when you want claude.ai or phone access. Most of the data-model, board and capture-flow design below is the researchers' inference. The sourced material covers the precedents, the provider and API constraints, and the MCP/auth facts.

## One job per concept: containers, context, state

The pain in the current app comes from using one concept for two jobs. PARA warns that treating areas as projects, such as listing "Hiring" as a project, hides work and makes progress hard to see. The reverse also holds: dumping ongoing work into a project means it never finishes ([Forte Labs PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)). GTD places Areas of Focus above Projects, where projects are created and eliminated, and users review each Area weekly as a trigger list ([GTD horizons summary](https://clients.intenthq.com/@gtdtricks/gtd-6-horizons-of-focus-by-david-allen)). That source is a community write-up, not David Allen's text. Things 3 puts Areas above Projects, lets both hold to-dos, and treats Today, Anytime and Someday as built-in states rather than containers ([Things docs](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803573/)). Todoist states the ownership rule plainly: a task lives in one project ("where does this belong") while labels are cross-cutting context such as people or energy ([Todoist](https://www.todoist.com/inspiration/todoist-labels-filters)).

From this the researchers infer a rule set. A task has at most one home, either a project (which belongs to an area) or an area directly, or neither (standalone). Area and Project are single-valued foreign keys. Tags are optional many-to-many metadata restricted to a small controlled vocabulary of context, energy and people. A tag must never duplicate an Area name, so there is no "japanese" tag once a Japanese Area exists. The "Workspace/Context" concept is redundant for one user and Area replaces it. The reliable sources cover Area/Project/tag semantics in PARA, GTD, Things and Todoist. The tag-deduplication rule itself is the researchers' synthesis, since no authoritative source on explicit dedup rules was found.

## A Focus board is a view over status, not a container

Personal Kanban's second rule is to limit work in progress, as a guideline that emergencies can override ([personalkanban.com](https://personalkanban.com/?p=129)). A developer-oriented guide suggests a Doing limit of 1 and a Ready limit of 5, and says Today should hold only cards you realistically will touch ([Super Productivity](https://super-productivity.com/blog/personal-kanban-for-developers/)). A GTD-forum thread reports 3 as the common personal WIP answer, treats Someday/Maybe as the Kanban backlog, and notes GTD does not forbid a Today list ([GTD forum](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/threads/merging-gtd-and-personal-kanban.12739/latest)). No source was found for your exact five-column set, so it is a design choice consistent with these practices rather than a documented standard.

The researchers' inference is that planning tools such as Sunsama treat Today as a view pulled from a backlog ([Sunsama](https://www.sunsama.com/compare)). So status should be orthogonal to area and project, and drag-and-drop only changes status. Suggested defaults are a soft cap of about 5 on Today and a warn-don't-block cap of 1 to 3 on In Progress. Done shows the last 7 days and then archives. Someday is excluded from default counts.

Ongoing Areas such as job hunting, Japanese and soft skills should never be cards on the board. This is inference, because no source covers ongoing learning goals. Two mechanisms replace it. Each Area carries a "next action" task so it never goes dark. Recurring tasks like "Japanese: 30 min" regenerate into Up Next or Today when completed or on schedule, instead of piling up in Done. Recurrence handling in Things, Todoist and TickTick was not verified from official docs, so this design is unvalidated.

## Propose-then-approve works with a flat draft and hard caps

Ramble shows the interaction pattern: capture box or voice, an instant draft of editable items, per-item removal and project or label change, and one commit button ([Todoist help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/use-ramble-to-add-tasks-by-voice-P1Raq7vVF)). Doist's engineering notes, as summarized by Product Talk, describe choices that transfer to a small solo app. They injected the full list of projects and labels into the system prompt instead of using RAG, injected the current date, and tuned for literal capture so the model records tasks rather than doing them. Their observed failure modes were date confusion, ambiguous project or label names, and the model executing instead of capturing ([Product Talk](https://www.producttalk.org/building-todoist-ramble-how-doist-turned-voice-braindumps-into-real-time-task-capture/)). Taskade drafts an outline the user can adjust before creation ([Taskade](https://www.taskade.com/automate/text-to-action/chat-message-to-project)), and Goblin.tools offers a granularity dial plus a per-subtask re-breakdown ([Sheridan libguide](https://sheridancollege.libguides.com/ai-tools-for-students-module/increase-productivity-with-ai/create-task-lists)). Whether Motion, Tiimo or Taskade hold generated plans in a true pre-commit state was not confirmed.

On the Next.js side, the AI SDK migration guide says `generateObject` and `streamObject` are deprecated in favor of `generateText` and `streamText` with `output: Output.object({schema})`. Failures throw `NoObjectGeneratedError`, and `elementStream` yields validated completed array elements, which suits streaming draft cards ([AI SDK migration guide](https://app.unpkg.com/ai@7.0.40/files/docs/08-migration-guides/24-migration-guide-6-0.mdx); [AI SDK structured data](https://ai-sdk.dev/docs/ai-sdk-core/generating-structured-data)). Provider support is uneven across your free-tier cascade, which drives schema design. Cerebras strict mode needs an object root, forbids recursive schemas and oneOf, and does not support `minItems` or `maxItems` ([Cerebras](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/capabilities/structured-outputs)). Groq's strict mode is limited to listed models, and other models may return malformed JSON or HTTP 400 ([Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/structured-outputs)). Gemini returns syntactically valid but possibly semantically wrong output and may reject deeply nested schemas ([Gemini](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output)). OpenRouter support is per endpoint, so `provider.require_parameters: true` is needed ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/structured-outputs)). For dates, chrono-node accepts a reference date and timezone and a `forwardDate` bias ([chrono-node](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/chrono-node@2.10.1/README.md)).

The researchers' inference from these constraints is a flat schema that fits the intersection of provider limits: `{summary, items:[{kind: area|project|task, title, tempId, parentRef (existing id | draft tempId | null), matchesExistingId|null, confidence, dueText|null, estimateMinutes|null, sourceQuote, rationale}]}`. The LLM returns the raw date phrase and the server parses it with chrono-node, so the model never does date arithmetic. Code verifies every `matchesExistingId` exists, runs a fuzzy title check as backstop, and enforces caps: at most 1 new Area, 3 new Projects and 10 to 12 tasks per capture, with depth fixed at Area > Project > Task. Items whose `sourceQuote` does not appear in the user's input are dropped as likely invention. Low-confidence items arrive unchecked. A "detail level" setting and per-item "break down further" replace one-shot deep trees. A schema failure triggers one retry that feeds back the Zod error, then falls through to the next provider. None of these mitigations has been tested, and no study of AI task apps specifically was found. The sourced backing is general: decomposition drift and hallucination over long plans ([arXiv 2605.04070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04070); [arXiv 2402.02716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.02716)).

## MCP: ship a bearer-token route first, add OAuth only on demand

Vercel's `mcp-handler` v2 mounts a stateless Streamable HTTP MCP server at an App Router path such as `app/api/mcp/route.ts`, exporting the handler as GET and POST. It needs Node 20+, `@modelcontextprotocol/server` v2 and zod v4 ([vercel/mcp-handler](https://github.com/vercel/mcp-handler)). Vercel's docs state that "The package does not issue tokens or run an authorization server." `withMcpAuth` returns 401 for a missing token and 403 for missing scopes, and a tool reads the user from `ctx.http?.authInfo` ([Vercel docs](https://vercel.com/docs/mcp/deploy-mcp-servers-to-vercel); [vercel/mcp-handler](https://github.com/vercel/mcp-handler)). That split decides the phase plan. Claude Code accepts a plain bearer header via `claude mcp add --transport http name URL --header "Authorization: Bearer ..."` ([Claude Code MCP docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp)). Claude's connector auth supports `oauth_dcr` and `oauth_cimd`, and `static_headers` is a beta limited to certain orgs and set by an org Owner ([Claude connector authentication](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/authentication.md)). So reaching claude.ai, Desktop or mobile means the planner must act as or front an OAuth authorization server, for example the Better Auth `mcp()` plugin ([Better Auth](https://better-auth.com/docs/plugins/mcp)). ChatGPT custom MCP requires remote HTTPS and manual confirmation of write actions, and plan eligibility sources conflict ([OpenAI MCP docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/mcp)).

Adopting an existing server is faster but splits the data. Todoist has an official hosted server at `https://ai.todoist.net/mcp` ([Doist/todoist-ai](https://github.com/Doist/todoist-ai)), and Notion, Linear, Asana and ClickUp have official OAuth servers. ClickUp's free tier is capped at 50 calls per rolling 24 hours ([ClickUp docs](https://developer.clickup.com/docs/connect-an-ai-assistant-to-clickups-mcp-server.md)). The researchers' inference is that these expose generic task models that cannot represent your Area > Project > Task hierarchy or your approval flow, so they are a fallback and not a destination. Zapier's free tier is about 100 tasks per month, with a per-call MCP draw reported by one blog only ([Latenode](https://latenode.com/blog/zapier-mcp-pricing)). Self-hosted n8n has no execution cap but needs hosting ([getorchestra](https://www.getorchestra.io/guides/n8n-pricing)).

The security posture is sourced in principle and inferred in detail. The MCP tools spec says servers must validate inputs, enforce access controls and rate limit, with a human in the loop able to deny calls ([MCP tools spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/server/tools)). Secondary sources describe the "lethal trifecta" of private data, untrusted content and external communication ([codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/how-to-secure-mcp-servers-2026/)). Task text returned to the model is untrusted, so the researchers recommend no outbound tools (no email, no URL fetch), capped batch sizes, `userId` scoping on every query, a write log with one-click undo, and a separate draft table so the model can never write directly into the real tables.

## Recommended architecture

### Data model (inference, modeled on Things 3 and Notion PARA relations)

```
Area    { id, name, color, sortOrder, archivedAt?, cadenceNote? }
Project { id, name, areaId? (nullable FK), status ACTIVE|ON_HOLD|DONE, targetDate?, doneAt? }
Task    { id, title, notes, status INBOX|TODAY|IN_PROGRESS|UP_NEXT|SOMEDAY|DONE,
          projectId?, areaId?, sortOrder, dueDate?, scheduledFor?, doneAt?,
          recurrenceRule?, estimateMin?, isAreaNextAction }
Tag / TaskTag   (replace JSON tags; small controlled vocabulary)
ProposalDraft { id, userId, rawInput, source, status PENDING|APPLIED|DISCARDED,
                items JSON, createdAt }   // never touches real tables until applied
```

A CHECK constraint ensures a task has either `projectId` or `areaId`, not conflicting values. Effective area equals `task.areaId ?? task.project.areaId`, and chips show the project if present, else the area. Indexes go on (status, sortOrder), (projectId) and (areaId). A project cannot be completed while child tasks are open, mirroring Things. Drop "Context/Workspace". Migrate by mapping each existing Context to an Area, and migrate JSON tags to Tag rows, flagging any that duplicate an Area name. Add an `userId` to all rows now if multi-device or OAuth access is ever planned.

### Focus board

One query returns tasks not Done (plus Done in the last 7 days), grouped by `status`, filtered by area, project and tag. Drag changes only `status` and `sortOrder`. Cards show a project chip, or an area chip for area-level tasks, a due date and tags. The six group-by modes are removed. Show WIP warnings for Today (about 5) and In Progress (1 to 3), without blocking. Completing a recurring task creates its next occurrence in Up Next.

### Capture flow

The sequence is: type a loose goal in a capture box, then the server builds a prompt with all Areas and Projects as `{id, name, description}`, the current date and timezone, a low temperature, and an "extract, do not execute" instruction. It calls `generateText` with `Output.object` and the flat schema, validates with Zod, retries once with the error fed back, then falls to the next provider. Post-processing verifies IDs, runs the fuzzy duplicate check, parses `dueText` with chrono-node, enforces caps and drops items lacking a source quote. The result is saved as a `ProposalDraft` and shown as editable cards with "matches existing: X" switches, per-item checkboxes, and a detail-level control. A single Approve button creates records in one transaction. Rejected proposals are logged for prompt tuning. Add a mic button using the Web Speech API as a progressive enhancement that fills the same textarea. Chrome sends that audio to Google ([Mozilla Hacks](https://hacks.mozilla.org/2016/01/firefox-and-the-web-speech-api/)). Anything arriving from outside channels such as Telegram lands as a pending draft and is never auto-created.

### MCP phase plan

| Phase | Scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Schema migration, Focus board, Areas UI | Prerequisite for everything else |
| 1 | In-app propose-then-approve capture (text, then mic) | Delivers decision 3 without MCP |
| 2 | `mcp-handler` route with long random rotatable bearer token, tools `list_board`, `list_areas`, `search_tasks` (readOnlyHint), `propose_plan` (writes a draft only), `apply_plan(planId)` with `idempotencyKey`, `move_task`, `update_task` | Works with Claude Code and MCP Inspector at near-zero cost |
| 3 | OAuth authorization server (Better Auth MCP plugin or hosted IdP), consent screen on the existing login, token `sub` mapped to `userId` | Only if claude.ai, Desktop, mobile or ChatGPT access is wanted; prototype first |
| 4 | Telegram bot or iOS Shortcut endpoint feeding the draft inbox, with allowed-user check and webhook secret | Needs an LLM key for free-text parsing |

Phase 2 and the tool list are design recommendations. The phase ordering, the draft-table safety property, and the "no outbound tools" rule are judgment, not sourced practice.

## Open questions for the user

The first open question is whether you want OAuth access at all (claude.ai, phone, ChatGPT), which decides whether Phase 3 is ever built. Next is whether tasks directly in an Area should be allowed to sit on the board indefinitely, or whether Areas should be forced to carry exactly one next-action task. The team also needs your answer on whether recurring study habits like Japanese should be recurring tasks, a weekly cadence target on the Area, or both. Confirm the WIP numbers, the Done retention window, and whether Someday tasks should surface in a weekly review. Decide how existing Contexts and JSON tags map to Areas and Tags, and whether any existing Projects are really Areas. Decide whether a new Area may ever be proposed by the AI or only suggested for your manual creation, and whether you want a per-capture cap different from 1 Area, 3 Projects and 10 to 12 tasks. Finally, decide which capture channels matter beyond the text box (voice, Telegram, email) and what you will tolerate in LLM API spend.

Capture and goal-breakdown UX for Notion AI, Saner.ai, Superlist, Amie, Lindy and Sunsama returned no usable data. No benchmark of free-tier model structured-output reliability was found, and Groq and Cerebras strict-mode summaries listed gpt-oss and Qwen models, not Llama, so confirm current model lists. Zapier and n8n figures come from third-party blogs, not pricing pages. Search-summarizer outputs for the AI SDK, provider docs and Anthropic's support article were not independently checked against raw pages. Sources on Todoist's missing refresh token, ClickUp's tool count and Claude routine caps are single or secondary. ## Conclusion

The three decisions you made are mutually reinforcing, and the key insight is that approval is the safety mechanism for all of them. Because a draft table sits between any AI and the real Area/Project/Task tables, the in-app capture box, a future MCP `propose_plan` tool and a Telegram message can share one pipeline and one review screen. That also makes the MCP phase cheap to start, since a bearer token and a handful of tools reach Claude Code today, and the expensive OAuth work becomes an optional later step tied to a real need.

The riskiest assumptions are not the architecture but the ones nobody has tested: that free-tier models produce acceptable flat plans, that the source-quote and cap heuristics curb invention without hiding real tasks, and that recurring tasks plus Area next-actions keep ongoing goals alive without cluttering the board. A small saved set of messy inputs, scored in the Doist LLM-judge style, would settle the first two before Phase 1 ships, and one real month of use would settle the third.
